## 1. Why this file exists

Eternal is a listed company that publishes quarterly shareholders' letters and hosts public earnings calls, so the evidence ceiling here is high — comparable to [Day 44 — Nykaa](../Day-44-Nykaa) and well above the private companies earlier in this series.

Unusually, **the single number this case study is built on is disclosed by the company itself**: Blinkit's inventory losses of **₹308 Cr in Q1 FY27, stated as 1.8% of Net Order Value**, with the CFO attributing a large part to perishable products. Almost no retailer volunteers this figure. It is the reason this case study exists in the form it does.

But a disclosed number is not the same as a decomposed one, and that gap is this study's soft spot.

**The short version.** The central thesis rests on five claims:

| Claim | Standing |
|---|---|
| Eternal's centre of gravity has moved from food delivery to quick commerce | 🟢 **Well-evidenced** — Blinkit NOV ₹17,132 Cr vs food delivery ₹10,769 Cr; Blinkit ~78% of revenue; and the Blinkit CEO became CEO of the parent on 1 February 2026 |
| Consolidated revenue growth of 173% overstates business growth, which is ~66% like-for-like | 🟢 **Well-evidenced** — management publishes both figures; the 1 September 2025 shift to an inventory-led model is documented |
| Blinkit's margin gap (0.6% vs a guided ~6%) is the entire equity story | 🟢 **Well-evidenced** — segment EBITDA and management guidance are both disclosed; 27 of 29 analysts rate the stock a buy on this thesis |
| Inventory loss of ₹308 Cr/quarter is a **large, addressable** margin pool | 🟠 **Partially evidenced.** The number and the perishable attribution are disclosed. **The recoverable share is not disclosed and is assumed** |
| Eternal has no product team that owns inventory realisation | 🔴 **Inferred, not evidenced.** Inferred from the complete absence of freshness, stock-age or markdown behaviour in the product. **No knowledge of Eternal's org chart informs this** |

The fourth and fifth claims are the case study's spine and its two weakest links. Both are flagged in the README — in §47, §51 (open question 1), §57 (risk 8), §64 and Appendix C — and the proposed rollout plan begins with a three-week analysis designed to kill the proposal if claim four is false.

4. **Consolidated revenue growth: +173% adjusted vs +182% reported vs +66% like-for-like.** Not a conflict but a definitional and accounting-model difference. **Like-for-like is used throughout.**

## 3. The load-bearing assumption

Everything in §46 through §56 of the README depends on one unverified proposition:

> **A material share of Blinkit's ₹308 Cr quarterly inventory loss is caused by predictable, slow-moving perishable stock, and can be recovered by changing what customers see and what they pay.**

**What supports it.** The CFO's own attribution of "a large part" to perishables. The structural fact that perishables have a hard expiry clock and quick-commerce baskets are perishable-weighted. Fifty years of supermarket markdown practice, which exists because it works. And the disclosed frequency strategy — small, frequent, fresh-heavy baskets — which is exactly the shape that generates this loss.

**What does not support it.** Eternal has not decomposed the loss by cause. If damage in transit, theft, handling and genuinely unforecastable demand collapse account for most of it, then demand steering recovers a fraction of what this case study assumes, and the ~60 bps margin claim in §39 and §50 falls to something much smaller.

**How the case study handles this.** It does not hide behind the uncertainty and it does not pretend to resolve it:

- §47 makes it the dominant term in the RICE sensitivity analysis, which produces a **seventeen-fold spread** in the score
- §51 makes it **open question 1**
- §53 makes **Phase 0** a three-week analysis with an explicit **kill criterion** — *"proceed only if predictable perishable slow-movement is a material share; if damage and theft dominate, kill the proposal"*
- §64 names it as the study's largest limitation

**A reader who believes most retail shrink is structurally unaddressable should reject the proposal, and this file is written so that they can.**

## 5. What would change the conclusion

| If this were true | The conclusion would change how |
|---|---|
| Damage, theft and handling account for most of the ₹308 Cr | The proposal should be dropped; the margin bridge runs through density and take rate alone |
| Store-level loss rates are uniform rather than long-tailed | The problem is structural, not addressable by targeting; the intervention gets much more expensive |
| Blinkit already runs internal markdown or stock-age ranking that is simply invisible externally | The case study's core claim is wrong. **This is the most likely way I am wrong and I cannot rule it out from outside** |
| Recorded shelf life is unreliable across most perishable SKUs | The feature works only for dated categories; scope and value both shrink materially |
| Fresh produce is a small share of Blinkit's basket | Reach in §47 drops sharply and the RICE score falls below the roadmap threshold |
| Food delivery's 5.6% NOV margin erodes under zero-commission attack | The capital plan changes more than anything in this case study; margin becomes a survival question, not an optimisation one |
