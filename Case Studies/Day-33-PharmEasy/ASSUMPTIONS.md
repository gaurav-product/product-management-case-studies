# Assumptions, Evidence Grades & Limitations
### Day 33 — PharmEasy | Companion document to `README.md`

**Purpose.** This case study mixes three very different kinds of content: figures taken from public sources, judgements I formed, and numbers I invented to make a product proposal concrete. This file separates them so no reader — recruiter, PM, or the companies named — has to guess which is which.

**Research window:** July 2026. **Method:** secondary research only. No primary interviews, no paid research reports, no internal data, no app-analytics subscription, and no hands-on audit of the PharmEasy app.

---

## 1. Evidence grading scale

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Company disclosure or primary filing, or reported consistently across 3+ independent credible outlets |
| **B** | Reported by 2 independent credible outlets, or one strong outlet (major business press) |
| **C** | Single source, or an aggregator/unlisted-shares site whose methodology is not published |
| **D** | Self-selecting or structurally biased sample — directionally useful, not measurable |
| **INF** | My inference or reconstruction from observable behaviour — explicitly not documentation |
| **PROP** | My original proposal — invented figures used to make a recommendation concrete |

## 6. User sentiment — read this before quoting the 1.7/5

| Claim | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ~1.7/5 across 248 reviews; ~85% unfavourable | **D** | PissedConsumer — a **complaint** platform. People who post there are, by definition, complaining |
| Complaint themes: late/missing delivery, wrong or expired items, refund failure, unreachable support | **B for pattern, D for level** | The themes recur across PissedConsumer, Trustpilot and MouthShut independently. That consistency is meaningful |
| Refunds 7–10 days | **D** | Individual complaint reports, not a measured SLA |

> ⚠️ **This is the evidentiary weak point of the whole analysis.** The claim "economics fixed, experience not" rests substantially on biased samples. A proper test would be app-store rating trend over time, a commissioned NPS study, or company-disclosed CSAT — none of which I have. **The thematic consistency is real; the 1.7/5 number should not be quoted as if it were an NPS.**

## 10. What I would do to raise confidence

In priority order:

1. **Read API Holdings' filed financials** rather than secondary reporting of them — fixes the single biggest limitation
2. **15–20 interviews with chronic patients** using PharmEasy vs Tata 1mg — replaces the weakest evidence (the sentiment claim) and would ground the personas in reality
3. **Place and track 20 real orders** across platforms, measuring promise-vs-actual — would turn my central argument from inference into measurement
4. **A 2026-current market-share estimate** from a paid research source — the 2023 figure is load-bearing and stale
5. **Instrumented accessibility testing** with screen readers — §27 currently overstates what a heuristic pass can conclude
6. **Better sourcing on quick-commerce GMV** — a strategically important number resting on one blog
