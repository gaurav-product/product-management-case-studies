# Assumptions Log — Google Ads Case Study (Day 29)

All items below are labeled **ASSUMPTION — VALIDATION REQUIRED** in the main README and are consolidated here per repository data-integrity convention.

1. **AI Max independent account-level testing result (84% of advertisers seeing neutral or negative outcomes)** — sourced from a practitioner/agency test set reported via secondary coverage. The full methodology, sample size, account-size distribution, and measurement window were not independently verified in this pass. **This is the weakest load-bearing data point in the case study**, and it does significant analytical work (it is the counterweight to Google's own +14%/+27% claim and the evidentiary basis for the §50 feature proposal). Treated as directionally credible but not settled; flagged in §30, §45, §64, and §65.
2. **Google's AI Max uplift figures (+14% typical, +27% for exact/phrase-keyword-heavy campaigns)** — Google internal data, self-reported, with no disclosed methodology, sample, or holdout design. Reported as a vendor claim, not as independent evidence.
3. **Technical architecture (§41)** — Google does not publish the architecture of its ad retrieval, ranking, and auction systems in meaningful detail. The described pipeline is a reasonable external model synthesized from public documentation and industry understanding, not documentation of Google's actual implementation.
4. **Google Ads advertiser verification rollout in India (all advertisers serving ads in India, from 2026)** — sourced from third-party practitioner service providers who perform this verification commercially. No corroborating Google Ads Help or Google blog documentation was located in this pass. Referenced in §33 and §40 with explicit labeling.
5. **Google Ads-specific accessibility (WCAG) conformance** — no product-specific accessibility conformance statement for the Google Ads console was located. Absence of located evidence is not confirmation of absence.
6. **Google Ads-specific security and privacy certifications** — no certifications specific to the Google Ads product (as distinct from Google Cloud's broader compliance portfolio) were located; flagged as a research gap rather than a confirmed weakness.
7. **EU ad tech decision date** — the €2.95 billion fine amount is consistent across sources, but the date is reported as both September 2025 and January 2026. September 2025 appears in more independent legal sources and is treated as more likely, but was not verified against the European Commission's own publication.
8. **Ad tech remedies ruling status as of July 25, 2026** — the most recent source located (June 18, 2026) states no divestiture order had issued and that Judge Brinkema was still drafting. Whether a ruling issued between that date and publication was not confirmable in this pass. Treated as unresolved and explicitly dated.
9. **Global digital advertising market size (2026)** — reported figures range from roughly $680B to approaching $800B depending on scope and definition, and the most accessible sources are aggregators restating IAB, eMarketer, WARC, Statista, and Dentsu rather than the primary reports themselves. Reported as a range, not reconciled to a single number.
10. **Emarketer platform share and revenue forecasts (Meta $243.5B / 26.8% vs. Google $239.5B / 26.4%)** — these are *forecasts* for a year still in progress, not reported results, accessed via secondary reporting rather than the Emarketer report itself. Emarketer also noted the forecast was finalized before certain 2026 court rulings. Directionally the most-cited figure in the industry, but not an actual outcome.
11. **Google Network revenue as a proxy for open-web health** — the Network line is used in §12, §36, and §45 as a financial indicator of third-party publisher monetization. It is an imperfect proxy: it reflects Google's own supply-side share as well as ecosystem contraction, which is precisely why the IAB/PwC programmatic growth figure (+20.5% in 2025) is presented alongside it rather than the Network decline being attributed solely to AI Overviews.
12. **Historical product dates (AdWords 2000 launch, 2002 CPC/Quality Score shift, 2003 AdSense, 2008 DoubleClick close at ~$3.1B, 2018 rebrand, 2021 Performance Max, 2023 Demand Gen)** — drawn from widely-corroborated industry record and Google's own retrospective accounts rather than verified against primary filings in this pass. Dates are well-established; exact figures (e.g. the DoubleClick purchase price) are as commonly reported.
13. **Universal Cart / UCP monetization model** — Google has not disclosed whether transaction-layer economics will be advertising-priced, fee-priced, or both. §18 and §39 state this as an open question rather than inferring a model.
14. **Business Agent for Leads advertiser controls and liability model** — no public documentation specifying advertiser control over, or liability for, generative agent output was located. §40 states this as an identified gap, not as a criticism of a known design.
15. **AI Overview / zero-click CTR figures** — the primary studies cited (Pew Research Center, July 2025; Seer Interactive, September 2025; Ahrefs, 2025–2026; BrightEdge, February 2026) use different methodologies, samples, and query mixes, and reported CTR declines across the wider literature range from roughly 15% to 89%. The direction is unanimous across studies; the magnitude is not. Specific figures are attributed to specific studies rather than averaged.
16. **Google Ads advertiser counts, revenue per advertiser, campaign-type mix, feature adoption rates, and churn** — none are publicly disclosed by Google. §32 treats this opacity as a finding rather than filling the gap with estimates.

**Verification standard applied:** All Q2 2026 financial figures were cross-checked across a minimum of three independent outlets reporting on the same Alphabet disclosure and reconciled against Alphabet's quoted CEO/CFO commentary. Product launches and feature descriptions were verified against Google's own blog and support documentation where available. Antitrust case status was cross-checked across five independent legal and trade sources. Market-share, market-size, and third-party measurement figures carry meaningfully lower confidence and are labeled inline in the README and consolidated above. Where two sources conflict, both are recorded in §65 Appendix A rather than silently reconciled.
